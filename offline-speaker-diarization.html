Offline Speaker Diarization for Windows

Speaker diarization separates a recording into speaker turns: who spoke when. Vidrick combines this with transcription on your Windows PC so you can review a conversation as labeled text.

Speaker labels are a starting point for review

A transcript can contain the right words but attribute them to the wrong person. Diarization helps organize the conversation, but an automatic label is not proof of someone’s identity. Check the recording before naming a speaker or attributing an important quotation.

Your audio and video recordings are transcribed locally on your Windows PC; nothing from those recordings is uploaded to the cloud for transcription. Speaker analysis is part of that local workflow. Account setup, downloads and license checks require internet access.

Automatic or a known number of speakers?

Use Automatic speaker identification when you do not know how many people speak in the file. If you know the expected number, enter it in the speaker options. Count people who actually speak, rather than everyone who attended the event.

An expected count helps guide the job; it cannot create clear voice evidence where the recording lacks it. Vidrick warns when the audio evidence cannot support the requested count. A very brief response, distant microphone or overlapping voices may still need manual interpretation.

Long recordings and uncertain speech

Vidrick 4.3 processes long recordings in sections and compares recurring voices across the recording. Speaker-analysis progress is shown while the work runs.

For long recordings, speech that cannot be matched confidently is marked “Uncertain speaker (review)” in exports. Treat this as a review category, not an additional person: it may contain speech from more than one participant. Return to the original audio before assigning a name.

A review checklist for interviews and panels

  1. Check the opening turns. Listen to each participant’s first clear contribution and compare the assigned labels.
  2. Sample later sections. Confirm that returning speakers keep the appropriate labels after long gaps or changes in microphone position.
  3. Listen around interruptions. Short replies and overlapping speech can be harder to assign than sustained speech.
  4. Resolve uncertainty before quoting. Review names, numbers and speaker attribution together. Correct the text in the transcript editor.
  5. Check the exported file. Export TXT or DOCX for written review, or SRT/VTT for subtitles, and verify the result in the tool where you will use it.

Example: a three-person discussion

If two guests and a host all speak, three is a reasonable expected count. If a guest only laughs or gives a short overlapping answer, the audio may not provide enough clear speech to separate that person reliably. Review those moments instead of assuming that every label or the requested count must be correct. This is an illustrative workflow, not an accuracy benchmark.

What affects the result?

Background noise, similar voices, microphone distance and simultaneous speech all make review more important. A clearer recording usually provides better evidence, but no setting guarantees perfect labels. Start with a short sample using the Windows transcription workflow. For a recording in another language, also check the language selection guidance.
