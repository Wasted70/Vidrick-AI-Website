Multilingual Offline AI Transcription for Windows

Vidrick AI Transcriber offers 57 named transcription-language choices plus Auto-detect in its Windows desktop app. Use it to create a working transcript of recorded speech, then review and export the result.

Choose the language you know was spoken

The selector includes English, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Arabic, Chinese, Hindi, Japanese, Korean, Russian, Ukrainian and Vietnamese, alongside other choices. These are transcription-language options, not a claim that every language, accent or recording produces the same accuracy.

If you know the recording language, select it directly. Use Auto-detect when you want the app to determine the language. Listen to a short sample and compare the output before committing to a longer job, especially when the beginning contains music, silence or speech different from the main recording.

Transcription and translation are different tasks

Normal transcription aims to write the speech in its spoken language. Translate to English aims to produce English text from supported speech. Choose the task according to what you need to read or publish.

For example, a recorded Spanish interview may need a Spanish working transcript for a bilingual editor, or an English translation for another reviewer. Treat this as a choice of output, not interchangeable evidence. Translation can alter phrasing and nuance; check important quotations against the original with someone who understands the language.

Translate to English does not mean translation into any chosen target language. Likewise, Auto-detect is not a promise of reliable language switching within a mixed-language conversation.

A practical evaluation workflow

  1. Choose a representative excerpt. Include normal speech, names and any specialist vocabulary from the recording.
  2. Select the language and task. Decide whether you need a transcript in the source language or an English translation.
  3. Set speaker options. Use Automatic speaker identification or an expected speaker count for a conversation. Read the speaker-label review guide.
  4. Review with language knowledge. Check names, numbers, negation and terms that matter to the meaning. Dialect, accent, audio quality and model/settings can affect the output.
  5. Edit and export. Correct the transcript and save TXT or DOCX for written work, or SRT/VTT for subtitles. Verify text and timing before publication.

The same local Windows workflow

Your audio and video recordings are transcribed locally on your Windows PC; nothing from those recordings is uploaded to the cloud for transcription. Account setup, downloads and license checks require internet access. Language selection does not turn the app into a cloud transcription service.

Vidrick works with recorded audio and video files. Processing time depends on the computer, recording length and chosen settings. Start with the Windows setup guide, or read what local processing covers before evaluating private material.

Can I publish the output without checking it?

Human review is necessary for both the text and the speaker labels. When you do not understand the source language, involve a qualified reviewer rather than assuming fluent-looking output is accurate. Keep the original recording available for your own review process.
